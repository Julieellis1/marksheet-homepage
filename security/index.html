Security & data protection

Student records deserve better than a shared Excel file.

Marksheet is built for the Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023: consent capture, role-scoped access and an audit trail on every sensitive record.

Role-scoped access

Proprietors, principals, teachers, bursars, students and guardians each see only what their role allows.

Full audit log

A record of who viewed or changed sensitive records, kept behind every result and setting.

Guardian consent capture

Consent is recorded on the platform before guardian and student data is used.

Records kept separate per school

Each school’s records are isolated from every other school’s, including across Multi-Branch groups.

Offline-first exams

Exam answers are stored on the school network and sync to the cloud once connectivity returns.

Your data stays yours

The school is the data controller. Export records as CSV, Word, PDF or Excel at any time.

Nothing deleted on lapse

If a licence lapses the platform soft-locks to read-only. Nothing is deleted without your request.

Tamper-evident results

Published report cards carry a unique code. The public portal shows the authenticated summary stored on the platform.

Confirm with Marksheet before launch: (1) the live site says “NDPR compliant”, but since NDPC’s GAID took effect on 19 September 2025 the NDPR no longer applies and the NDPA 2023 is the law, so update the claim and say whether a compliance audit return has been filed; (2) where data is hosted and how often it’s backed up; (3) whether a Data Protection Officer is named; (4) the registered company name and RC number for the legal pages.

Common questions

Data, plainly.

How is student data protected?

Guardian consent capture, role-scoped access so every screen shows only what that person is meant to see, and a full audit log of who viewed or changed sensitive records. Each school's records are kept separate from every other school's.

Who owns our school's data?

Your school does. The school is the data controller for its own records and can export them as CSV, Word, PDF or Excel at any time.

What if the internet drops during an exam?

Exam delivery and multiple-choice marking run on the school's local network with no internet at all. Answers are saved on the school's machine and sync to the cloud once the connection comes back.
